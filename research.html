Cognitive Achievement Distributions and Adult Wage Returns: Evidence from NELS and ELS

Summer 2026 · R, Stata

Public attention has centered on falling national test scores for students since the 2020 pandemic. In fact, the bottom half of these scores have been dropping since the mid-2010s (NCES 2024). To the extent there is any relationship between standardized test scores and adult wages, this recent change in student performance is cause for concern regarding wage inequality. That said, the conventional wisdom, based largely on findings from two cohorts of students from 1979 and 1997 (Castex and Dechter 2014; Deming 2017), has suggested that the relationship between test scores and adult wages has weakened over time, which could mitigate some of the concern about rising disparities in test scores. This study analyzes a more recent set of students: 10th graders in 1990 and 10th graders in 2002, with different results. Specifically, the relationship between test scores and adult wages in this sample strengthened significantly over time for women, with no evidence of decreases for men. Through a statistical decomposition of these estimates, the growth in returns to women’s test scores appears to be primarily driven by increased wage returns for the bottom half of scorers. These results highlight the need for additional research and policy attention to focus on low-scoring students, whose recent test-score declines threaten to widen disparities in both cognitive achievement and labor-market outcomes.
